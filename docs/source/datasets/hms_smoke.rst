NOAA HMS Smoke Polygons
=======================

Analyst-drawn smoke plume outlines from NOAA's Hazard Mapping System, produced daily since August 2005.

Overview
--------

The Hazard Mapping System (HMS), first implemented in 2002, is NOAA's analyst-driven fire and smoke product: satellite analysts outline the smoke plumes visible in satellite imagery and record the satellite and the time window of each outline.

The smoke polygons describe where smoke was visible, which fire detections and perimeters do not; they are commonly used to flag smoke-affected days and to link plumes to fires.

   * - Formats
     - Zipped shapefiles (daily files and annual bundles), KML, annual GeoTIFF smoke-day rasters

Data Characteristics
--------------------

- Attributes are Satellite, Start, End and Density (Light, Medium, Heavy or NA).
- The density attribute is consistently filled only from 1 June 2010; most earlier files have "NA" (apart from April-December 2008), so density-based analyses should start in mid-2010.
- File names and columns changed on 19 July 2022, when NOAA regenerated the historical archive; daily shapefiles are named hms_smokeYYYYMMDD.zip.

Typical Use Cases
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

- Flagging smoke-affected days and regions in air-quality or wildfire impact studies.
- Linking smoke plumes to fire detections and perimeters.
